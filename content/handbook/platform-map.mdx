## Problem

Without a map, every AI discussion collapses back to model choice.

## Symptoms

- Different teams solve gateway, evals, guardrails and observability differently.
- The same failure is rediscovered in multiple products.
- Leadership sees AI as a feature queue, not an operating surface.

## Mental model

Use cases flow down into infrastructure, and production feedback flows back up into product and model decisions.

## Metrics

Each layer has its own metric: use-case adoption, gateway coverage, provider reliability, inference utilization, cache hit rate, eval pass rate, trace completeness, safety event rate and ownership coverage.

## Trade-offs

Maps simplify reality. The goal is not to freeze architecture but to make missing responsibilities visible before they become product incidents.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating RAG, agents or model serving as isolated projects.
- Drawing a diagram without owners.
- Ignoring cost and quality loops until after launch.

## Checklist

<Checklist
  items={[

    "Cost, quality and safety loops are first-class layers.",
    "Operations and incident response are not hidden in infra."
  ]}
/>

## Example

When an agent loop gets expensive, the map points to several likely layers: tool schema stability in cache, routing in gateway, evals in quality gate, trace fields in observability and budget policy in operating model.

## Decision template

Before building a new feature, mark which platform layers it touches and which layer owns the release risk.
