## Problem

Teams often confuse the most visible component with the system. In AI, that visible component is the model.

## Symptoms

- The roadmap says "upgrade model" but not "change release process".
- Product teams compare benchmark charts without scenario evals.
- Nobody can answer who owns cost, incidents, fallback or quality regression.

## Mental model

Production AI is a platform responsibility. The model is a component behind contracts, telemetry, quality gates, budgets and operations.

## Architecture

The platform owns the stable interface between product use cases and model execution: API contract, model aliases, policy, routing, prompt lifecycle, observability, quality and cost.

## Metrics

Measure accepted outcomes, regression rate, fallback rate, cost per scenario, incident count, cache hit rate, release lead time and ownership coverage.

## Trade-offs

Centralization can become a bottleneck. A good platform does not approve every prompt; it provides golden paths, tools, contracts and guardrails so product teams can move safely.

## Anti-patterns

- Direct provider calls from every product team.
- "Model research" treated as "model lifecycle".
- Guardrails bolted on after incidents.
- Cost reviews that ignore retries, fallbacks and failed outcomes.

## Checklist

<Checklist
  items={[
    "The platform thesis is visible on the first screen.",
    "Model replacement is possible without product rewrites.",
    "Every production scenario has quality and cost telemetry.",
    "Every release has rollback or fallback.",
    "The platform team publishes reusable paths, not only support tickets."
  ]}
/>

## Example

A new transcription summarizer should not hardcode one model and one prompt in a product service. It should request a scenario through a platform API, get a model alias, emit telemetry and pass a quality gate before broader rollout.

## Decision template

Use this sentence: "This is a platform concern because it affects more than one model, team, scenario or release."
